His Eminence

Archbishop Makarios of Nairobi

Born Andreas Tillyrides in Limassol, Cyprus, on April 18, 1945, he is a Cypriot bishop of the Greek Orthodox Church of Alexandria.[reference:0]

His journey from a small Mediterranean island to the heart of Africa is a story of divine calling, scholarly excellence, and unwavering commitment to the Orthodox faith. After completing his PhD in Church History at Oxford University under the supervision of Bishop Kallistos Ware, he was sent to Nairobi in 1977 by Archbishop Makarios III of Cyprus to establish a Patriarchal Seminary in Riruta.[reference:1][reference:2]

Though he arrived as a layman, his heart was immediately stirred. As he later reflected: “A longing to stay and serve, a desire to know the people of the land.”[reference:3] That longing became a lifelong mission.

A Life of Service

Key Milestones

1977

Sent to Nairobi by Archbishop Makarios III to establish the Patriarchal Seminary in Riruta.[reference:9]

1981–1992

General Dean of the Patriarchal Seminary; developed liturgy translations for 15+ African languages.[reference:10]

July 1992

Tonsured a monk, ordained deacon and priest, and consecrated first Bishop of Riruta — all in one week.[reference:11][reference:12]

1998 – 2001

Elected Archbishop of Zimbabwe (1998) and later transferred to the See of Nairobi as Archbishop of Kenya (2001).[reference:13]

His Enduring Impact

Legacy & Writings

Under Archbishop Makarios' direction, the Orthodox Church has translated liturgical texts into languages that had never been written before.[reference:14] He oversaw the consecration of the first two native Kenyan bishops: Athanasius (Akunda) of Kisumu in 2015 and Neophytos (Kongai) of Nyeri in 2016.[reference:15]

He has authored hundreds of theological and scientific articles and has been a member of international theological dialogues with the Roman Catholic Church, the Reformed, and the Lutherans.[reference:16][reference:17]
